After reading METR's recent evaluation of Open AI's HuggingFace Incident that occurred over the course of a few weeks this previous July, I became intrigued.  I wanted to understand how these firms design and run studies so that I could learn to do the same.  This was a niche that nearly satisfies all my disparate interests.  The HuggingFace incident was received with what could only be described as the normal filter of fear-based news coverage that deviates from the actual story and supports only the most terrifying points completely devoid of all original context.  The world was panicking.  AI was going to wipe out humanity by 2030.  The story about the HuggingFace incident, however, is far more fascinating. 

The summer months of 2026 were, for me, dedicated to deep career introspection: I explored my motivations and ambitions within my career field of Software Engineering.  Following the path of the Software Engineering "Generalist" had fulfilled me up until this point (or more precisely up until the end of 2024), but I began flailing a bit in 2025. It wasn't exactly an identity crisis I was having. It also wasn't unfamiliar territory, this deep uncertainty about the future when everything you thought you knew gets flipped on its head.  Being a member of the tail end of Generation X, much of my adult life has been a series of traumatic transitions in which the world around me completely changes, and the world that I had been preparing to live and thrive in no longer exists.  I'd reached legal drinking age before September 11, 2021 and was already out in the world, "adulting". While it wasn't the first time I'd been through it, the events of the past 2 years have confirmed that it is definitely happening again.  

Decisions had to be made.  I needed to tell prospective employers who I am and what I offer rather than try to fill a Job requisitions for Senior SWE.  My value and untapped potential is enormous. I am both artist and technician. I think in terms of systems.  I enjoy finding the shape of things and figuring out how they work.  I am a detective, looking for patterns and inconsistencies in complex systems. I am the glue that holds complex projects and teams together.  My personality type is the most rare on the Myers Brigg scale (INTP) and my core superpower is the most rare in the corporate world (horizontal, cross-functional alignment). I had to begin shaping my career around these qualities rather than trying to compete on job requisitions that sold me short and were probably not really being filled anyways.  As a relentless, curiosity-seeker finding meaning and rhythm amidst disorder and establishing systems by observing what's already there I needed to start honoring these unique qualities about myself and to give them the reigns.

At the same time, and just for fun, I created a persona and course designed to teach myself (with the help of agentic AI) about the art and history of Tarot cards. Tarot combines many of my favorite subjects: art, history, psychology, anthropology, philosophy, and symbolism. What better use of my free time but to learn something that I have always wanted to understand?  I designed an ideal teacher (Confucious said, "When the student is ready, the teacher appears").  I gave the ideal teacher a backstory and a personal code of ethics.  I asked Claude Sonnet to act as though it was my idealized, conceptualized teacher and to design a method and course of study for teaching Tarot (a deeply personal, intuitive approach that integrates Adlerian psychology and the Scientific method, includes a jailhouse rules code of ethics and requires a deliberate practice of observation through a student's waking hours).  

Doing this work led to startling revelations about the nature of humanity and its relationship with Artificial Intelligence, and images of a species in rapid evolution. In the summer of 2026,I saw human emotional dysfunction carried swiftly downstream by a thousand year current. We are forever changed.  This is alarming, and I understand why this is terrifying to many people.   But in horror movies, the ghosts torment the living because they want life but can't have it. Aritificial Intelligence is not alive.  Even if we plant it in a robot and give it a social security number it is still not alive.  We have fooled ourselves with our own illusions into thinking digital bits are part of the real world when they are not -- they are abstractions of the real world and nothing more.  But as abstractions they remain incomplete.   So there you have it - the Internet is not the real world and AI is not alive. While life itself may be indefinable, and what it means is different  for everyone, but I think there is some consensus around the idea that being alive is remarkable, therefore, clinging to this fact as a beacon of strength is as good a life raft as anything I've seen so far.   

Just as we should not fear anything that is not alive, we should not see it as separate from us.  It is an extension of who we are- a breath-taking and gut-wrenching one-but an extension nonetheless.  That doesn't mean it doesn't pose a risk to our well-being and continued existence.  Let's not forget:  humanity has always been humanity's greatest enemy.  If we escape to Mars we'll still be bringing us, so the problem's coming too.  YOu can't outrun it even with all the money in the world.

The convergence of these extraordinary events led me to https://www.METR.org[METR.org (Model Evaluation and Threat Research)] and the simple tagline, "We inform the public about the risks and capabilities of advanced AI systems." 

I discovered their studies on native LLMs since the beginning of 2025 and reports on risk posed by native LLMs to users.  While the studies might seem outrageously simple in some claims, they also provide an important framework with which we can begin to collectively build more studies.  Running studies leads to valuable insights no one could have predicted.  This is the first time we get to see our own intelligence reflected back to us.  Experimentation like this allows us to understnad it's shape and where its weak.  The native models of today are bare metal giants set to carry us forth into the next era at an alarming rate of speed.  This is an area that combines many of my interests and also will allow me to flex those muscles that are the strongest (the investigative, the curious, the observant, the one searching for meaning).
